# Yuggenda API - Permissions Model

## 1. Roles per Business

* `OWNER`
* `ADMIN`
* `STAFF`

Customers are not business members and therefore do not receive business management roles.

---

## 2. Permissions by Role

### Owner

* Can manage the business.
* Can manage business members.
* Can assign and modify member roles.
* Can manage services.
* Can manage staff availability.
* Can manage customers and appointments.
* Can perform sensitive business operations.
* Cannot remove the last `OWNER` from the business.

### Admin

* Can manage business resources.
* Can create, edit, and deactivate services.
* Can manage staff availability.
* Can manage customers and appointments.
* Can manage business members within the permissions allowed by the system.
* Cannot perform owner-only operations such as transferring or removing the last owner.

### Staff

* Can view relevant business resources.
* Can manage their own availability.
* Can view and manage appointments assigned to them.
* Can work with services they are assigned to.
* Cannot manage business members or modify business-level permissions.

---

## 3. Customer Permissions

### Registered Customer

* Can access their own customer information.
* Can view their own appointments.
* Can create appointments where permitted.
* Can cancel or reschedule their own appointments according to business rules.
* Cannot access other customers' data.
* Cannot manage business resources.

### Guest Customer

* Can create an appointment without an account.
* Can provide the information required for the booking.
* Does not receive authenticated access to the business management system.

---

## 4. Additional Rules

* A user must be a member of a business to access its protected management resources.
* Business members can only access resources belonging to their associated business.
* Staff can only manage appointments and availability within their assigned business.
* Customers can only access their own permitted customer and appointment data.
* Sensitive operations must be protected by ASP.NET Core authorization policies and appropriate role checks.
* Authorization must be enforced on the server and must not rely on client-side restrictions.
